# Introduction to AI Agents

AI is changing the way we work.

Modern AI systems can do much more than answer questions or generate text. They can increasingly take on meaningful pieces of work, interact with digital environments, use tools, complete multiple steps, and produce finished outputs.

This shift introduces an important concept:

> **AI Agents**

Understanding how to work with agents is becoming an increasingly valuable skill because they can help us spend less time on repetitive work and more time on activities that require human judgment, creativity, problem solving, and decision making.

---

## What is an AI Agent?

An **AI Agent** is an AI-powered system that can pursue a goal and take actions to accomplish it.

Instead of only telling you how to complete a task, an agent can potentially perform parts of that task for you.

An agent may be able to:

* Understand a goal
* Interact with a digital workspace
* Open and review documents
* Use tools
* Take actions
* Create outputs
* Check its progress
* Continue working across multiple steps

This is one of the major differences between a traditional AI chatbot and an AI agent.

---

# Chatbot vs AI Agent

The easiest way to understand agents is to compare two different ways of asking AI for help.

Imagine that you are an accountant responsible for checking a large number of client invoices.

Each invoice is expected to contain a valid tax ID.

---

## Approach 1: Ask AI for Instructions

You could ask:

```text
What should I check to determine whether an invoice is missing its tax ID?
```

The AI might explain:

* Where tax IDs are normally located
* What a valid tax ID looks like
* What should be considered missing

This information is useful.

However, you would still need to:

1. Open every invoice.
2. Locate the tax information.
3. Check whether the ID exists.
4. Identify the invoices with missing information.
5. Create your own list of exceptions.

The AI explained **how to perform the task**, but you still had to perform the work yourself.

---

## Approach 2: Give the AI the Task

Instead, imagine asking:

```text
Review this folder of client invoices,
identify the invoices that are missing a tax ID,
and create a summary spreadsheet containing the flagged invoices.
```

An AI agent could potentially:

1. Open the folder.
2. Read the invoices.
3. Check the required information.
4. Identify invoices with missing tax IDs.
5. Create a spreadsheet.
6. Add the flagged invoices.
7. Provide the completed output.

The goal is still the same: identify invoices with missing tax information.

The difference is what the AI does after receiving the instruction.

---

# The Key Difference

With a traditional chatbot:

```text
User asks a question
        ↓
AI provides information
        ↓
User performs the work
```

With an AI agent:

```text
User provides a goal
        ↓
Agent performs multiple steps
        ↓
Agent interacts with tools or information
        ↓
Agent produces an output
```

A useful way to remember the difference is:

> **A chatbot can explain the work. An agent can help carry out the work.**

Modern AI products increasingly combine both types of capabilities, so the distinction between chatbots and agents is becoming less obvious.

---

# Agents Can Perform Multi-Step Work

One of the most important characteristics of AI agents is their ability to work across multiple steps.

A traditional chatbot interaction may look like this:

```text
Question
   ↓
Answer
```

An agentic workflow may look more like:

```text
Goal
 ↓
Review Information
 ↓
Use Tools
 ↓
Take Action
 ↓
Check Progress
 ↓
Take Another Action
 ↓
Create Output
```

This allows agents to work on tasks that would otherwise require a person to move between several applications or complete several repetitive activities.

---

# Example 1: Managing a Family Schedule

Imagine managing a busy family calendar containing:

* School schedules
* Classes
* Sports practice
* Music lessons
* Medical appointments
* Your own calendar
* Pickup and drop-off responsibilities

Normally, you might need to compare several schedules manually.

You could instead give an agent the schedules and ask it to organize the week.

The agent could:

1. Review everyone's schedule.
2. Determine who needs to be where.
3. Compare different commitments.
4. Identify scheduling conflicts.
5. Find available time slots.
6. Organize everything into one schedule.

For example, it might discover that two pickup responsibilities happen at the same time on Thursday.

Instead of spending significant time manually comparing calendars, the agent helps organize the information and identify the problem.

---

# Example 2: Client Meeting Follow-Up

Consider what happens after a client meeting.

A person may normally need to:

* Review the meeting notes
* Write a follow-up email
* Update the customer record
* Create tasks
* Assign follow-up actions

These activities can take additional time after the actual meeting.

An agent could receive the meeting notes with an instruction such as:

```text
Complete the follow-up activities from this client meeting.
```

The agent could then:

1. Review the meeting notes.
2. Prepare the follow-up email.
3. Update the client's record in the CRM.
4. Create the required project tasks.

This allows the person to spend more time focusing on the client relationship and the next important activity instead of administrative work.

---

# Example 3: Monitoring Customer Support

Agents do not always need to begin with a manually entered request.

Imagine an agent that runs every morning before the support team starts work.

The agent could:

1. Review the support inbox.
2. Review the service status information.
3. Look for unusual increases in support tickets.
4. Identify repeated issues.
5. Summarize the pattern.
6. Share the finding with the team.

For example:

```text
7:00 AM
   ↓
Review Support Inbox
   ↓
Review Status Information
   ↓
Identify Ticket Pattern
   ↓
Summarize Issue
   ↓
Post Summary to Team
```

Instead of starting the day by manually searching through many different signals, the support team can begin with the important problem already identified.

This can help teams respond to smaller issues before they develop into larger problems.

---

# What Can AI Agents Do?

From these examples, we can see that agents may be able to perform activities such as:

* Reviewing information
* Reading documents
* Interacting with digital workspaces
* Using tools
* Identifying patterns
* Completing multiple steps
* Creating outputs
* Updating systems
* Checking progress
* Continuing until a task is completed

The exact capabilities depend on the tools and environment available to the agent.

---

# From Answering Questions to Completing Work

Earlier AI tools were primarily used through conversational interfaces.

A typical interaction was:

```text
Ask AI something
      ↓
Receive an answer
```

Agents introduce a different interaction model.

```text
Give AI a goal
      ↓
AI performs work toward that goal
      ↓
Receive the completed output
```

This changes the role AI can play in everyday work.

Instead of using AI only as a source of information, people can increasingly use AI to help execute workflows.

---

# Agents and Long-Running Tasks

Some tasks require much more than a single action.

They may involve:

* Opening several files
* Reviewing information
* Comparing details
* Taking multiple actions
* Creating outputs
* Checking whether the goal has been achieved

Agents can increasingly work across these longer sequences.

Tasks that might normally require many hours of manual work can potentially be broken down and performed through agent-driven workflows.

The important concept is that the agent continues working **toward an objective**, rather than stopping after producing a single response.

---

# Why AI Agents Matter

Many workdays contain a large amount of routine or mechanical work.

Examples include:

* Checking information
* Organizing schedules
* Updating systems
* Writing follow-up messages
* Reviewing repetitive documents
* Monitoring operational information
* Creating summaries
* Completing administrative tasks

These activities are necessary, but they consume time and attention.

If agents can take responsibility for more of these activities, people can spend more time on work that requires uniquely human capabilities.

Examples include:

* Thinking
* Creating
* Solving difficult problems
* Exercising judgment
* Evaluating trade-offs
* Developing ideas
* Making decisions

---

# AI as a Way to Expand Human Capability

The value of AI is not only about completing the same amount of work faster.

It can also change what a person is capable of taking on.

Consider a professional whose day is filled with repetitive tasks.

```text
Available Time
      ↓
Routine Work
      ↓
Limited Time for Higher-Value Work
```

If some of that routine work can be handled by agents:

```text
Agent Handles Routine Work
          ↓
More Time and Attention
          ↓
Thinking
Creating
Problem Solving
Decision Making
```

This means AI can potentially increase not only productivity but also the range of work a person is able to pursue.

# The Line Between Chatbots and Agents Is Blurring

It is becoming increasingly difficult to divide AI applications into simple categories such as:

```text
Chatbot
```

and

```text
Agent
```

Modern AI applications are increasingly adding agentic capabilities.

A conversational AI system may still answer normal questions while also being able to:

* Use tools
* Work with files
* Perform tasks
* Take actions
* Complete multi-step workflows

Because of this, it is more useful to understand the **capabilities of the system** rather than focusing only on what the product is called.

---

# Using AI vs Directing AI

There is an important difference between simply using AI and learning how to direct it effectively.

Using AI might mean asking:

```text
Explain this topic.
```

Directing AI might mean giving it responsibility for a larger objective:

```text
Review this information,
identify the important issues,
complete the required actions,
and prepare the final output.
```

As agent capabilities improve, knowing how to clearly direct AI becomes increasingly important.

---

# Direction Becomes an Important Skill

Traditionally, one of the major limits on professional output has been execution.

A person only has a limited number of hours in a day.

```text
Your Time
    +
Your Ability to Execute
    =
Your Available Output
```

Agents can change that relationship.

Instead of personally executing every repetitive activity, a person can increasingly direct AI systems to perform parts of the work.

This makes **direction** extremely important.

The question becomes:

> How clearly can you direct an agent toward a useful problem?

Being able to identify meaningful problems and clearly describe the required outcome becomes an important skill.

---

# A Manager Analogy

One useful way to think about working with agents is to compare it to managing people.

A strong manager does not personally perform every task completed by their team.

Instead, the manager:

1. Provides direction.
2. Explains the desired outcome.
3. Establishes expectations.
4. Allows others to execute the work.

Once a process has been explained effectively, that knowledge can continue producing value.

AI agents introduce a similar idea.

Instead of repeatedly performing the same task manually, you can define how the work should be performed and allow an agent to execute the process repeatedly.

---

# Turning Repetition Into Training

Imagine that you perform the same administrative process every week.

```text
Week 1 → Perform Process
Week 2 → Perform Process
Week 3 → Perform Process
Week 4 → Perform Process
```

You repeatedly spend time executing the same steps.

With an agent, the approach can begin to shift toward:

```text
Explain the Process
        ↓
Agent Learns the Required Direction
        ↓
Agent Performs Repetitive Work
        ↓
Human Focuses on Higher-Value Activities
```

The idea is to turn repeated execution into reusable direction.

---

# Compounding Benefits

When repetitive work can be handled consistently by agents, several potential benefits appear.

These include:

* Faster turnaround
* More consistent execution
* Increased capacity
* Less time spent on repetitive work
* More time available for higher-value activities

The important benefit is not simply completing one task faster.

The larger benefit comes when the same type of work can be delegated repeatedly.

---

# What Should Humans Focus On?

As agents become capable of handling more mechanical work and routine reasoning, human effort can increasingly focus on areas that require deeper judgment.

These include:

### Weighing Trade-Offs

Choosing between different options when there is no perfect answer.

### Exercising Judgment

Understanding context and determining what should actually be done.

### Creating

Developing something new rather than simply following an existing process.

### Solving Harder Problems

Focusing attention on problems that require deeper thinking.

### Deciding What Is Worth Doing

AI may help perform a task, but humans still play an important role in deciding which problems deserve attention in the first place.
