# Prompt Engineering and Context Engineering 

## 1. Overview

AI models and agents can perform impressive tasks: help manage work, automate repetitive activities, and even create attractive web pages or apps. Yet two people using the same AI model may get very different results.

This introductory lesson asks a practical question:

**Why do some people get much better results from AI tools than others, and how can we learn to do the same?**

The course proposes that a major part of the answer is understanding **how AI produces responses** and **which inputs and surrounding components influence those responses**.

You do **not** need to be a programmer to begin learning these ideas.

## 2. An Everyday Example

Imagine two people using the same AI tool to create a website.

- **Person A** asks: “Make me a website.”
- **Person B** explains who the website is for, what it should contain, the desired style, and what a successful result looks like.

Person B has given the AI more useful direction. This example is illustrative—it is **not** an experiment or example presented in the source video.

The larger point is that AI output depends partly on how the request and surrounding information are supplied.

## 4. Two Big Ideas: Prompt Engineering and Context Engineering

The presenter summarizes the course as an exploration of **prompt engineering** and **context engineering**.

### Prompt Engineering

**Simple meaning:** Improving the instructions or questions you give an AI system to obtain more useful results.

An illustrative example:

**Basic prompt:**

> Explain AI agents.

**More directed prompt:**

> Explain AI agents to a beginner in simple English. Use one everyday example and explain how an AI agent differs from a normal chatbot.

Both requests ask about AI agents, but the second gives clearer direction about the intended audience and output.

### Context Engineering

**Simple meaning:** Organizing the information and surrounding inputs available to an AI system so it has what it needs for the task.

For example, a person preparing an AI assistant to answer questions about an organization's support policies might supply the relevant policy material and explain how the assistant should use it.

**Important distinction:** The video names context engineering and refers to “hidden components,” but does **not yet define those components in detail**. The explanation and example here are beginner-friendly illustrations, not quotations from the lesson.

### How They Relate

- **Prompt engineering** helps you communicate what you want the AI to do.
- **Context engineering** helps ensure the AI has the relevant information and surrounding setup for doing it.

They complement each other, but neither guarantees a perfect answer.

## 5. What Is Token Prediction?

The introduction says the course will explain **token prediction** as part of how models generate responses.

For a beginner, a *token* can be thought of as a small piece of text that a language model processes. When generating text, language models predict tokens step by step based on the input and what has already been generated.

This is a short background explanation added to make the topic approachable. **The supplied introductory clip does not walk through token prediction itself.**

## 6. Why Can the Same AI Model Seem Better or Worse?

The presenter raises an important observation: a model may appear exceptionally capable in one interaction and much less capable in another.

The course will investigate how **the nature of the task** and **what is supplied to the model** contribute to those differences.

That does not mean a better prompt can make a model capable of anything. Model limitations and task difficulty still matter.

## 7. What Are AI Agents?

The video mentions people building agents that act as assistants or automate frustrating work.

A simple way to think about an AI agent is a system that uses an AI model as part of a workflow to perform actions or complete tasks, potentially using tools along the way.

## 8. Why This Matters for Everyday Users

The main message of the introduction is **accessibility**.

The presenter argues that understanding AI systems should not require a technical career. People can start with basic concepts and gradually develop the knowledge and judgment needed to direct AI tools more confidently.

The potential benefits include:

- Communicating tasks more clearly to AI models.
- Understanding why an output missed the mark.
- Becoming more intentional when setting up AI-assisted work.
- Exploring automation and AI assistants with greater confidence.
- Making better use of existing subject-matter knowledge.

These are learning goals and possible benefits—not outcomes measured in the video.

## 9. Useful Beginner Vocabulary

| Term | Plain-language meaning |
|---|---|
| **AI model** | A system trained to recognize patterns and produce outputs, such as text. |
| **AI agent** | An AI-powered system set up to carry out tasks, often using additional tools or steps. |
| **Prompt** | The instruction, request, or question given to an AI model. |
| **Prompt engineering** | Improving how requests and instructions are written. |
| **Context** | Relevant information and setup available to the AI for a task. |
| **Context engineering** | Shaping and organizing that information and setup. |
| **Token** | A unit of text processed by a language model. |
| **Token prediction** | The process of selecting the next token during text generation. |
| **Output** | The response or result produced by the AI. |

*These definitions are supplemental explanations for beginners; the introduction does not formally define every term.*
